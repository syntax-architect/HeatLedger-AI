## Slide 2: The Problem — The 1 October 2026 Regulatory Wall
- **The Trigger:** 
  - Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1963 requires all steel imports under CN 7208–7306 to prove **country of melt and country of pour** directly on an EN 10204 MTC.
  - Regulation (EU) 2023/956 (CBAM) enters definitive enforcement with default emission markup penalties (10% in 2026, 20% in 2027, 30% in 2028).
- **Who Pays:** European steel importers pay the CBAM certificate price (€75.36–€82.32/tCO2 in 2026). If an Indian supplier cannot supply clean MTCs and actual emissions data, the importer faces punitive default charges or simply cancels the contract.
- **The Plant Pain:** Plants process 50–200 MTCs per month manually across disparate formats, leading to mistyped heat numbers, unreadable chemistry, over-allocated tonnage, and delayed customs clearance.

## Slide 3: What We Do — The Evidence Engine
- **Instant Ingestion & Extraction:** Multi-pass OCR engineered for degraded, skewed Indian mill test certificates with confidence-scored heat, chemistry, and tensile extraction.
- **Buyer-Spec Check (Task C1):** Real-time tolerance verification against ASTM, EN, and SAE standards using ASTM E29 rounding-off.
- **Melt-and-Pour Custody Chain (Task C2):** Links mill heat numbers to plant production lots, shipment lines, and commercial invoices.
- **CBAM Exposure & Data Pack (Task C4):** Computes exact difference between plant actual emissions and punitive EU default values with dated quarterly certificate prices.
- **Tamper-Evident Sealing:** SHA-256 byte-identical hashing under EN 10204 Clause 6 rules with inspector step-up MFA.
